# Route Hacker product memo

## Product position

Route Hacker is a beta route-planning and fare-discovery tool designed to help travellers find valid flight combinations and understand route feasibility before booking.

It is not a full-price flight search engine and it is not positioned as a universal live fare service across all markets.

The product is useful when the user needs to explore possible combinations, identify schedule-valid connections, and understand whether a route is a realistic option given timing, fare coverage, and transfer risk.

---

## What the product does well

- finds possible route combinations from a given origin and destination
- surfaces schedule-based paths that may not be obvious in standard search tools
- highlights tight or risky connections
- shows route-level pricing signals and uncertainty where data is partial
- helps the user understand whether a route is viable before committing to a booking flow

---

## What it does not do yet

- does not guarantee a complete live-price feed for every route in every market
- does not claim universal coverage across all airlines or date windows
- does not promise final booking pricing for every result
- does not replace full mainstream flight search or OTA inventory coverage

---

## Product honesty standard

We will communicate the product as a beta tool with clear limitations.

Public-facing language will make it explicit that:
- some routes are partial or estimated
- some pricing requires confirmation
- some data may be missing or not fully verified
- not all combinations are guaranteed to be currently bookable or priced

The user should never have to guess whether a fare is final or whether the route is fully covered.

---

## Release status

### Private beta

This is the current and recommended stage.

Use private beta to validate:
- route quality
- price honesty
- user trust
- warning language
- failure patterns
- conversion and retention on supported markets

### Limited public beta

Possible only when the product is clearly branded as beta and coverage is transparent.

Requirements:
- visible beta badge and disclaimers
- price provenance on results
- clear risk warnings for tight/self-transfer routes
- limited access or waitlist model
- traffic protections and monitoring
- legal/privacy basics in place

### Full public launch

Not appropriate until the business can support stable data coverage, hosting, and operational cost for the relevant route markets.

---

## Business reality

The product is viable as a beta route-planning tool, but it should not be positioned as a fully complete flight booking product until the data strategy and business model are real.

It is acceptable to launch in a limited form when the team can support:
- real infrastructure
- stable route quality
- honest coverage messaging
- fit-for-purpose pricing language
- legal/privacy compliance

If those conditions are not met, the right move is to stay private.

---

## Recommended positioning

Recommended framing:

> Route Hacker is a beta route-planning tool for discovering valid flight combinations and understanding route feasibility. Some routes are estimated or partially priced and should be treated as guidance rather than guaranteed final booking offers.

This is honest, useful, and credible.

---

## Final recommendation

The product should remain a private beta until the data-cost model and coverage strategy are stable.

A limited public beta is possible only with a clearly disclosed beta status and a consistent pattern of honest pricing and coverage language.

The team should not attempt to market the product as a full live flight search engine while route coverage and pricing completeness remain partial.
